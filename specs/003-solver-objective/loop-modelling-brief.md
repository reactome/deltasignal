# Brief: how should DeltaSignal model feedback loops?

**Written** 2026-09-18, as a self-contained statement of the open problem.
Hand this to anyone (or any model) to get a second opinion without needing the
session history.

## The goal

Model positive and negative feedback loops so they behave as biology expects,
with parameters that can be **learned** from perturbation data.

Stated intent: positive and negative feedback loops should behave the way they
would biologically, with associated parameters that can be learned so the model
aligns with biological data. No particular model form is required.

## What the system is

- Networks derived from Reactome: bipartite entity/reaction directed graphs.
  92 pathways, ~334,844 edges. Generated by a separate Python pipeline.
- A **fold-change algebra**, not absolute concentrations. Baseline x0 = 0.01
  internal (= "1x normal"), range [0,1] internal = 0-100 UI.
- Per reaction: activators combined (AND = product of fold-changes capped at
  100x; OR = mean), inhibitors divide (`H = baseline/x`), result clamped.
  One reaction per target node; a node produced by several reactions is
  OR-combined into one.
- Current solve: Tarjan SCC condensation. Acyclic components evaluated exactly
  once in reverse topological order. Cyclic components get a damped
  Gauss-Seidel sweep to a fixed point. Observations are pinned hard.
- **No rate constants exist in Reactome.** Any dynamic parameter must be
  learned; time would be in arbitrary units.

## The scale of the loop problem

- **30 reaction-level cycles across all 92 pathways** — 2 recycling-obscure,
  16 recycling-explicit, 4 negative feedback, 8 positive feedback.
- They explode into **251 entity-level SCCs covering ~18,000 nodes** through
  variant multiplicity (one stable id can have 384 UUIDs). Small count, wide
  reach.
- Classification is only well-posed at the **reaction** level (precedingEvent
  is a reaction-to-reaction annotation). Three successive entity-level
  formulations gave 49%, 18% and 0.4% "real", and the middle one was
  DFS-order-dependent. Do not redo entity-level classification.

## What is measured and settled

1. **The solve is not a function of the graph.** Renaming every UUID (verified
   isomorphic — identical stable-id edge multiset for 92/92 pathways) moves
   predictions. Five labellings span **0.47pp** accuracy. Cause: node indices
   come from Julia `Dict` order, which drives the Gauss-Seidel sweep order,
   which selects which root a cyclic component lands in. Zero acyclic pathways
   churn.
2. **More iterations makes it worse.** At 20x the budget, two labellings
   diverged from 14 to 25 differing predictions. There is no unique root to
   converge to.
3. **The all-zero state is a global minimum** of `||F(x) - x||^2`. Gradient
   exactly 0, L = 0 — tied with the correct answer. A loop fed by a supply at
   baseline can sit at 1e-28 forever, and the solver reports
   `converged = true` at residual 5e-10.
4. **Minimising the designed objective LOSES.** `L = mu*||F(x)-x||^2 +
   gamma*||x-x0||^2`, box-constrained, Levenberg-Marquardt, warm-started from
   the fixed point. Held-out vs the fixed point: gamma=1e-6 **-71** (1 fixed /
   72 broke), 1e-4 **-87**, 1e-2 **-148**. All p < 0.0001, distributed over
   many pathways and readouts. Accuracy 83.36% -> 82.85 / 82.61 / 82.48.
5. **How it fails:** 136 of 137 broken cases had truth NORMAL and the fixed
   point got them right; the minimiser drifts them off baseline (103 DOWN,
   33 UP). Mostly small drifts (1.0 -> 0.816) crossing the band edge, only 4%
   full collapses. That is **false change**, already 48.2% of all errors.
6. **Performance is solved.** LM over the sparse Jacobian: 0.226s on an
   840-node component (LBFGS: 29s and still drifting), 814s for the full
   catalog — same wall as the fixed point. ForwardDiff differentiates the
   propagator cleanly through its `min`/`max`/`clamp` branches.

## Why (4) happened — now CONFIRMED (see specs/014-loop-elasticity)

AND is multiplication of folds, so every edge has elasticity 1 and a positive
cycle has **loop gain exactly 1 at baseline**. Baseline is a knife-edge, not a
stable state. On a two-reaction AND-loop, `U = 1.01` drives A to 1.09 / 2.30 /
100.0 at 50 / 500 / 5000 sweeps; `U = 0.90` collapses it to 0.0026 and reports
converged. The answer to a small perturbation is a function of the iteration
budget — which is (1), (2), (3), the false-change concentration, and the
minimiser's loss (it finds the rails exactly) all at once.

**Fix under test:** elasticity `ε < 1` on cycle-closing activator edges only
(`DS_LOOP_ELASTICITY`). Makes the loop's baseline root unique and stable
(`A = U^(1/(1−ε⁴))` on the fixture, matched to four figures), zero unstable,
strong drive still rails, in-loop knockout still collapses, acyclic edges
bit-identical. This is the design doc's Section 2.1 sensitivity α(x), which
shipped inert at `s = 0`. Catalog A/B running.

Corollary that still stands: for positive feedback, **multiple roots are the
biology** (bistability); the defect was that baseline was not one of the
*stable* ones.

## Dead ends — do not re-run these

- Loop-type classification at entity level (order-dependent, meaningless).
- `DS_INHIBITOR_FLOOR` as an anti-collapse device (tested, did not help).
- Blaming loops for the accuracy gap: the 17pp cyclic-vs-acyclic gap is a
  **between-pathway confound**; within pathway it vanishes. Same for readout
  in-degree, saturation, path length, and "readout downstream of a cycle"
  (78.8% vs 60.2% raw, **+0.7pp** median within pathway, 11 of 21 pathways
  positive). Ten case-level signals have died this way.
- gamma-continuation (anneal gamma down): implemented, and the
  baseline-connected branch descends INTO the collapsed state.

## Constraints on any proposal

- **Identifiability.** 334,844 edges vs ~23,000 scored cases, and those cases
  are far more correlated than they look (18 "independent" discordant cases
  once turned out to be one readout seen eighteen times). Learn per-edge-TYPE
  first (catalyst, assembly, depletion, diagram_bridge, input, output), not
  per-edge.
- **Acyclic behaviour must not change.** It is exact and correct today, and is
  the large majority of every network.
- **Measure on the wide curator set with the held-out split** (tune on the
  paper's ten pathways, report on the other 70). The 9-pathway experimental
  set has reversed five decisions in this project.
- Report both ground-truth axes, and report how many pathways and distinct
  readouts a gain spans — a coverage fix produces a cluster that looks
  distributed and is not.

## The concrete open question

Propose a representation of cyclic components that (a) makes positive feedback
latch and negative feedback attenuate, (b) carries learnable per-edge-type
parameters, (c) leaves acyclic solving untouched, and (d) can be validated on
synthetic motifs with known-correct behaviour before any catalog run.
